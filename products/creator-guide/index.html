Library and toolkit

01Unit · Foundation

From idea to a clear content project

Don't start by buying a camera. Choose a specific audience, a recurring problem and an honest teaching promise.

  1. A measurable outcomeExample: I help small business owners film an understandable product video with their phone.
  2. Map the episodeThe idea in one sentence, then the proof or test, then three scenes that serve it, then a close with the next step.
  3. Every shot has a reasonEach shot has to add information or a feeling.

Exercise

Write five problems your audience has. Pick one and write two openings: a direct question and a visual test. Film both and compare how clear the idea is, not just the views.

02Unit · Camera

Exposure and camera control

Aperture, shutter and ISO are three ladders. Each step doubles or halves the light, and each has a different side effect.

  1. The exposure triangleAperture sets light and depth of field, shutter sets motion blur, ISO sets signal gain and noise.
  2. Frame rate and shutterStart at 1/50 for 25 fps, 1/60 for 30 and 1/100 for 50: a starting point, not a law.
  3. Balancing lightIn bright light with a wide aperture, use an ND filter instead of a faster shutter, and watch the histogram.

Exercise

Lock the camera and film a moving hand at 25 fps twice, at 1/50 and 1/250, compensating exposure. Compare the motion blur and note the conditions.

03Unit · Camera

Lenses, composition and camera movement

A lens is not just zoom: focal length with sensor size and distance changes the angle of view and how a face looks.

  1. Shot sizesFrom extreme close-up to wide: the frame's bottom edge decides what the viewer sees.
  2. CompositionLeave room for looks and movement; break the rule of thirds when centring or symmetry serves the meaning.
  3. Movement with a reasonA push-in raises importance, a pan reveals information, and a locked shot can be the strongest.

Exercise

Film the same sentence as a locked shot, a slow push-in and a side angle. Ask someone to describe the feeling without telling them your intent.

04Unit · Light

Lighting: from a window to a studio

The character of a shadow comes from the apparent size of the light as seen from the subject, not from the price of the lamp.

  1. Soft and hardA large, close source is usually softer than a small, distant one; a diffuser alone does not guarantee softness.
  2. Inverse squareFor a point source, doubling the distance gives a quarter of the light. Do not apply it literally to a large, close softbox.
  3. Colour temperatureKelvin roughly describes warm and cool. Set white balance and avoid mixing unlike sources by accident.

Exercise

Film three shots at the same exposure: side window, front softbox, side light. Note source position, distance and the shadow.

05Unit · Sound

Sound: diagnose before you process

An expensive mic in an echoing room can sound worse than a suitable mic close to the mouth.

  1. The room before add-onsReduce reflections from hard surfaces and move away from noise sources.
  2. Distance and directionPlace the mic close but outside the plosive zone, and use its pickup pattern.
  3. The processing chainA clean level without clipping, gentle clean-up, high-pass, corrective EQ, moderate compression, then delivery level.

Exercise

Record the same sentence at three distances and listen on headphones to your voice against the room noise, before any processing.

06Unit · Story

Visual storytelling and planning

Define the problem, show what is at stake, show the test, then the result and its limits. Clarity first.

  1. The shot listA-roll to explain, B-roll for proof and detail; write why each shot exists, its length and its sound.
  2. ContinuityWatch eyelines, hand movement, object positions and where the sound sits.
  3. The 180-degree ruleChanging the camera angle alone does not rescue a confusing cut.

Exercise

Make a 45-second video about one lighting mistake: problem, test, comparison, fix. Plan the shots before recording.

07Unit · Editing

The art of cuts and transitions

Every cut is a story decision: it keeps the motion, links a meaning or lets sound lead the picture.

  1. Cut on actionCut during a clear action and keep the logic of movement and direction across both shots.
  2. J-cut and L-cutThe next scene's sound starts before its picture, or the previous sound carries on after the picture changes.
  3. Fade and dissolveNot a default for every cut; depending on context they can suggest time passing or calm.

Exercise

One product, two 30-second versions: straight cuts, and many transitions. Show both without a title and ask which is clearer and why.

08Unit · Editing

Editing in DaVinci Resolve

An organised project first, then meaning, then rhythm, then sound and colour, then an export checked on a phone.

  1. Project managementMedia, Audio, Graphics and Exports folders, clear names, and the right frame rate before building the timeline.
  2. Sound and colourBalance speech and music; start colour with white balance, exposure and matching before any LUT.
  3. Export and reviewCheck safe text areas, audio artefacts, fonts and compression on a phone and another screen.

Exercise

Build a first cut: order the meaning, set the rhythm, add B-roll where it proves something, then export a test and review it on two devices.

09Unit · Growth

Writing Reels and keeping viewers

Open with a clear problem or proof, and never promise a result you cannot show. No opening formula guarantees reach.

  1. The openingThe first image and first line stop the scroll when they match what the video actually shows.
  2. Rhythm that serves the ideaCut repetition but leave time to understand a comparison; more cuts is not proof of quality.
  3. AnalysisWatch retention, average watch time, saves and shares, and never infer cause from a single video.

Exercise

Test two openings for the same idea, keeping everything else as fixed as you can, and record the results and what you cannot be sure of.

10Unit · Systems

AI inside the workflow

Use AI to gather questions and draft, and verify numbers, specifications and rights against the original sources.

  1. Verifiable researchSeparate what the source confirms, what is inferred and what still needs checking.
  2. What we don't doNo claiming tests we didn't run, no generated images presented as real tests, no faces or voices without rights.
  3. A complete workflowFrom idea to analysis and improvement, with human review before publishing.

Exercise

Ask an AI for ten ideas, pick one, and split its claims in a table into proven facts and hypotheses that need testing.

11Unit · Business

Personal brand and digital product

Trust before selling: who the product is for and not for, real samples, and clear limits on the result.

  1. Trust before sellingExplain what the buyer gets and the limits of the result, and show real samples.
  2. Deliberate pricingA low price does not guarantee sales. Test perceived value, support, refunds and tax first.
  3. Records and rightsKeep a log of licences for photos, music, fonts and screenshots, and check platform terms.

Exercise

Write a 150-word sales page: the problem, the skill gained, a sample, the product scope and one common question, with no inflated promises.

12Unit · Practice

Final project and a 30-day plan

Four weeks from choosing a topic to publishing and analysing, then remaking the best idea with one specific improvement.

  1. Success criterionThree videos with documented settings, one before/after comparison and a short learning report.
  2. A test cardThe question, device and settings, what you fixed and what you changed, the result, limits and the next decision.
  3. Pre-publish checklistClaims, sound, exposure, text readable on a phone, rights and the export.

Progress check

Success is never tied to a guaranteed number of followers or views.

Library · 13–14

A prompt is a work brief, not a magic sentence

The R-C-T-F-Q form: role, context and audience, task, format, and quality bar with verification. Then 60 templates ready to adapt.

  • Split the work into stages and approve each one
  • Attach reference material instead of guessing
  • Keep a prompt log: version, inputs, model, output quality

Prompt 01 · Audience discovery

Analyse the following audience description: [description]. Suggest 12 practical problems that can be explained in a video, rank them by urgency and by how visually the fix can be shown, and say which ones need interviews with the audience.

Replace the text in [brackets] with your real information, and never publish the output without review.
